## Experiment 27 — `xau-state-v2` feature freeze

Experiment 27 freezes a richer raw-event state representation before the next unseen market window. It makes no entry, exit, sizing, priority or router change and selects no feature threshold from trade P&L.

`xau-state-v2` contains 20 continuous features: normalized 60 s range, spread and 10 s activity; ER60 and spread/range load; direction-normalized 10/30/60/300 s momentum; direction-normalized path efficiency; 10 s and 60 s raw flow alignment; short-vs-long momentum acceleration/balance; 60 s and 300 s pullback depth; market heat; execution heat; and session age. All rolling reference distributions use completed, lagged buckets only.

The formulas were defined structurally before outcome profiling. Across 319 known historical Candidate D trades and 71 known recent trades, all short-horizon state fields had 100% finite coverage. The 300 s-derived fields had 96.9% historical and 95.8% recent coverage because the causal baselines require warm-up after gaps.

Cross-grid stability remained strong. On 119 historical matched entries, the median feature Spearman correlation between 500 ms and 1 s was 0.916; 17/20 features were at least 0.80 and 11/20 at least 0.90. On the recent 27 matched entries, the median was 0.940; 18/20 features were at least 0.80 and 14/20 at least 0.90. The weakest features were pullback60 and spread-relative state; they are retained as continuous context, not standalone gates.

Descriptive PRIMARY profiles continue to support the admission/exhaustion hypothesis without creating a threshold. Recent max-hold winners occurred with lower median market heat than stop/zero-cross failures, while acceleration and pullback context also differed. These observations are **not** selection evidence because both windows are already known.

### Pre-registered Router v2 direction

The next router must evaluate engines as opportunity generators rather than globally good/bad modules. If a higher-priority engine is rejected by its state score, the simulator should continue to lower-priority eligible engines instead of automatically deleting the trade opportunity. HOLD remains valid only when no engine has demonstrated a sufficiently strong state.

For the first unseen-window Router v2 evaluation, the target is: higher net P&L and win rate than Candidate D on both grids, at least 95% system-level trade-count retention (or replacement opportunities that keep total count within 5%), no material drawdown deterioration, and preserved cost robustness. No router is promoted until a genuinely new non-overlapping snapshot is evaluated.

## Experiment 28 — stronghold-aware Router v2 implementation

Experiment 28 implements the first actual Router v2 mechanics while keeping Candidate D's engine signals, lifecycle, sizing, spread handling and risk exits unchanged.

When flat, the router no longer stops at the first fixed-priority signal. It gathers every currently eligible PRIMARY, SECONDARY, MICRO and BURST candidate, attaches the frozen `xau-state-v2` state, computes an engine-specific structural stronghold score, and selects the highest score. If the best candidate is below neutral, the router falls through to the next candidate; if none clears neutral, it emits HOLD.

The score definition is frozen before unseen evaluation. Each engine uses seven equal-weight components with no P&L weight search. Direction-normalized momentum/flow, pullback-to-favorable-edge, range/activity expansion and execution/market heat are combined according to the engine's semantic role. Continuous ratios are centered at their causal rolling baseline (ratio 1 = neutral), directional momentum uses a fixed tanh squash scale of 3, and the qualifying score floor is exactly 0.0. Ties preserve Candidate D's legacy engine priority.

The implementation includes a legacy-priority parity mode so Candidate D's lifecycle can be checked segment-by-segment before Router v2 diagnostics. Known data can be used for that implementation check only; it cannot be used to change weights, the squash scale, score floor or component list.

At the Experiment-28 freeze point, no Router v2 performance result had been accepted. The pre-registered promotion target was higher net P&L and win rate than Candidate D on both grids, at least 95% system-level trade retention, no material drawdown deterioration and later execution-cost stress. Experiment 30 subsequently supplied the full **known-data** raw-tick diagnostic and did not promote the frozen score; genuinely unseen non-overlapping data is still required before any future router can be promoted.

Evidence: `docs/experiments/2026-09-25/28-v4-5-router-v2-implementation.md` and `results/simulations/2026-09-25-v4_5-router-v2-implementation/`.

## Experiment 31 — Router v2 arbitration divergence audit

Experiment 31 makes **no strategy change**. Its instrumented Candidate-D and Router-v2 engines reproduce Experiment 30 exactly, then trace every arbitration divergence on the canonical first80 and known recent 24-hour data.

Only 48 decision divergences occur across both grids and windows. Direct same-time score substitutions are not large enough to explain the failure: their fixed-₹500 isolated Router-minus-D delta is only -₹2.33 historically at 500 ms and -₹8.80 at 1 s.

The dominant mechanism is path dependence. A changed engine or HOLD alters engine-specific cooldowns and shared-slot occupancy; that changes later entry timing and which opportunities exist. On the historical 500 ms 0-40% segment, the exact -₹353.97 segment gap decomposes into -₹148.85 from Candidate-D-only trades, -₹74.81 from Router-only losses, -₹51.06 from same-engine timing shifts and -₹79.25 from lower balance-based sizing on 90 otherwise identical trades. The immediate simultaneous substitution in that segment contributes ₹0 realized difference by itself.

The clearest HOLD examples rejected profitable PRIMARY entries with scores only slightly below zero: fixed-₹500 exact-entry counterfactuals of +₹61.39 at score -0.01635 and +₹43.16 at score -0.03317. But losing rejected entries occupy the same negative-score region. Lowering the floor to recover those winners would therefore be an outcome-fitted threshold, not supported generalization.

At 1 s the main 0-40% loss is also timing/occupancy driven: a +₹28.33 Candidate-D MICRO trade is replaced by a -₹22.46 Router SECONDARY trade 28 seconds later, a -₹50.79 swing.

The recent window confirms both sides of the mechanism. At 500 ms the Router gains ₹12.64 mainly by omitting two losing Candidate-D PRIMARY tickets, but at 1 s three Router-only follow-on trades lose -₹32.52 and erase the avoided-loss benefit.

Therefore Candidate D remains the V4.5 leader and the frozen Router-v2 score remains unpromoted. The next architecture should preserve Candidate D priority, define causal signal episodes so rejected opportunities cannot simply re-enter later as the same episode, and account for shared-slot opportunity cost before fallback. Do not sweep the known score floor.

Evidence: `docs/experiments/2026-09-25/31-v4-5-router-v2-arbitration-audit.md` and `results/simulations/2026-09-25-v4_5-router-v2-arbitration-audit/`.

## Experiment 33 — independent engine opportunity atlas

Experiment 33 makes no strategy change. It evaluates PRIMARY, SECONDARY, MICRO and BURST in independent one-position shadow lanes using fixed ₹500 sizing, removing shared-slot competition, cross-engine cooldown cascades and compounding from the labels.

## Lock direction

A strong V4.5 lock candidate should move toward:

- roughly 20% or better 500 ms capture if achieved without fragility;
- materially reduced 500 ms -> 1 s degradation, with ~10% 1 s capture treated as an aspirational robustness level rather than a forced threshold;

- no material PF or drawdown deterioration;
- improvement or containment of the weak 50-60% regime;
- positive cost-stress behavior.
