### BURST admission

BUY requires:

- PRIMARY / SECONDARY / MICRO all decline;
- cooldown >= 60 s;
- 10 s momentum >= +$0.60;
- 30 s momentum >= +$1.50;
- 60 s momentum >= +$2.00;
- 120 s momentum positive;
- ER60 >= 0.12;
- 10 s / 30 s quote-rate ratio >= 1.20;
- directional 10 s raw-tick imbalance >= +0.20;
- 60 s range >= $4;
- spread <= 18% of 60 s range;
- absolute spread <= $0.28.

SELL is symmetric.

### Candidate B lifecycle

- emergency stop: $4;
- take profit: $12;
- max hold: 120 s;
- 30 s momentum zero-cross failure exit;
- stagnation after 60 s if peak favorable move < $0.80;
- trail after +$5.50 with $2 give-back.

## V4.5-C Phase C1 — completed negative result

Phase C1 kept Candidate B admission unchanged and searched BURST lifecycle behavior for better profit velocity and lower sampling fragility.

It tested 48 combinations of:

- max hold: 120 / 180 s;
- trail trigger: $5.50 / $6.50 / $7.50;
- trail give-back: $2.00 / $2.50;
- optional raw-flow continuation extension;
- optional flow-decay early exit.

All 48 seed variants reproduced Candidate B exactly at both 500 ms and 1 s.

The top eight chronological evaluation candidates and top three full first-80% summaries also reproduced Candidate B exactly.

Therefore Phase C1 produced **no distinct Candidate C** and no promotion.

The result indicates that these lifecycle controls are shadowed/inactive on the observed BURST trade paths. Earlier BURST exit conditions and/or entry timing dominate before the tested knobs can change realized outcomes.

## Experiment 18 autopsy and provisional Candidate D

The parity-gated BURST autopsy found every Candidate B BURST trade exited on the first 30 s momentum zero-cross: 17/17 at 500 ms and 12/12 at 1 s. This exit precedes the Phase C1 lifecycle controls. The autopsy also found 28 BURST threshold episodes at 500 ms versus 25 at 1 s; six 500 ms episodes had no matched 1 s episode. Seven actual 500 ms BURST trades lacked a matched 1 s BURST trade. The whole-system 1 s loss is larger than the BURST component, with PRIMARY and SECONDARY contributing most of the summed-segment degradation.

Candidate D changes exactly one BURST lifecycle rule: a 30 s momentum zero-cross must persist for **one second** before it causes a failure exit; the timer resets if momentum recovers or is unavailable. Admission, engine priority, V4.4 engines, bid/ask accounting, the 3% planned risk, $4 stop, shared slot and other BURST exits remain Candidate B rules. One second was selected on the 0–40% seed because it improved P&L on both grids; 2/3 s full-run variants and a 5 s seed rejection are preserved in Experiment 19.

## Experiment 20 — exposure and sampling diagnostic

PRIMARY and SECONDARY use most of Candidate D's exposure. At 1 s, nine unmatched PRIMARY trades lost ₹174.04 across 2.46 exposure hours, and 22 unmatched SECONDARY trades lost ₹141.28 across 1.86 hours. Those matching categories are heuristic and do not prove a causal admission defect.

Experiment 20 tested conditional release at 120/300 s, two-second SECONDARY persistence, and a bounded 10 s momentum / quote-acceleration veto ablation on the 0–40% seed. None improved the joint 500 ms and 1 s profit-velocity frontier. For example, m10-only veto raised 1 s seed P&L from ₹169.92 to ₹317.74 and cut exposure, but reduced 500 ms seed P&L from ₹696.84 to ₹418.10. No variant progressed to later evaluation or cost stress. Candidate D remains provisional; no paper version is created.

The 50% profit-velocity, 20% exposure-reduction and 20% compounded-P&L gains are a **preferred strong-lock region**, not automatic promotion thresholds. A future candidate may be lock-eligible pending review after a narrow miss if its aggregate Pareto improvement is material. Minor PF/drawdown deterioration may be tolerated only when clearly compensated, documented and reviewed. A seed-only gain never locks V4.5.

Experiment 21 then tested an independent, risk-budgeted, at-most-three-ticket trend/impulse/reversal research architecture. Its initial full first-80% prototype lost at both grids, and two seed revisions failed joint-grid selection. It did not alter the single-slot Candidate D architecture or earn a paper version.

Experiment 22 froze a separate recent MT5 24-hour quote snapshot. Historical V4.4 golden parity passed on both grids before the fresh-data replay. Candidate D's realized-only recent seed result was -₹92.48 / -₹86.54 at 500 ms / 1 s, mostly from PRIMARY. The separate multi-ticket mode 1's trend tickets also lost on both seed grids. One seed-derived impulse-only ablation (mode 3) yielded +₹3.73 / +₹7.92 on seed but **-₹1.83 / -₹1.81 on the later evaluation**, worsening to -₹6.47 / -₹3.37 with an extra $0.20 adverse fill per side. The continuous 24-hour mode 3 result was only +₹1.89 / +₹6.08 before cost stress. This was far short of ₹100 per reported quote day and was rejected for promotion. The MT5 tick clock was about three hours ahead of host UTC; date labels in this snapshot are not verified civil UTC.

The fresh single-slot figures are realized-only and may exclude an open position at a segment boundary, while the independent ticket simulator forces a close. This limits cross-architecture ranking, but the mode 3 rejection is supported by like-for-like later evaluation and cost-stress comparisons within its own simulator. Candidate D remains the **provisional historical research leader, not locked**. The recent negative regime makes a V4.5 lock less supportable, not more. V4.4 remains locked and the final holdout sealed.

## Experiment 23 — raw-event regime-normalization foundation

Experiment 23 changes no entry, exit, sizing or risk rule. It introduces a diagnostic market-state layer calculated from the underlying raw tick stream **before** 500 ms or 1 s execution-grid sampling. The frozen representation uses lagged relative range/volatility, spread, path efficiency and quote activity rather than P&L-selected absolute regime thresholds.

V4.4 golden parity, Candidate B trace parity and Candidate D parity passed on both grids. Candidate D reproduced +₹1,430.31 / 163 trades / 81 wins at 500 ms and +₹385.25 / 156 trades / 71 wins at 1 s. Across 119 same-engine/same-side entries matched within 120 seconds, the raw-event categories agreed between grids on volatility 92.44%, spread 89.92%, efficiency 92.44%, activity 95.80% and the complete four-part regime key 76.47%.

The four-hour engine audit is diagnostic rather than a permission table. BURST was positive in 9 versus 2 negative 500 ms windows and 6 versus 4 at 1 s. MICRO was 10/6 and 10/5. PRIMARY was 10/10 and 9/9 with a negative median four-hour P&L on both grids despite positive aggregate P&L. SECONDARY was 6/8 and 7/9 and its 1 s aggregate P&L was -₹51.80.

No engine is disabled from the same dataset that revealed these weaknesses. Replay `xau-raw-event-regime-v1` unchanged on non-overlapping recent/future snapshots before selecting a deterministic regime router.

## Experiment 21 — independent multi-ticket horizon branch

An alternative from-scratch research script tested up to three independent, same-direction XAUUSD tickets under one 3% aggregate planned-risk cap. The script chose longer trend or shorter impulse/reversal horizons from past-only canonical features; each ticket had its own executable-price profit confirmation, $4 emergency stop and time limit. This was **not** a change to Candidate D or the locked V4.4 implementation.

The initial full first-80% prototype lost ₹22.14 / ₹52.92 at 500 ms / 1 s. Two bounded seed revisions also failed joint-grid selection: +₹29.15 / -₹23.13 for an active impulse lane and +₹13.72 / -₹37.90 for a reversal lane. None attained ₹100 realized P&L on an observed UTC quote day. Cost stress worsened each 1 s result. No multi-ticket prototype is promoted or lock-eligible. Candidate D remains provisional. Experiment 22 added a fresh snapshot check and a rejected impulse-only ablation.

Evidence: `docs/experiments/2026-09-24/21-v4-5-multi-ticket-horizon.md` and `results/simulations/2026-09-24-v4_5-multi-ticket-horizon/`.

## Search discipline

- first 80% research pool only;
- 0-40% used for search/selection;
- later chronological segments used for evaluation;
- final 20% holdout remains sealed;
- 3% planned risk remains fixed;
- $4 emergency stop remains fixed unless a future documented research plan explicitly changes it;
- one shared position slot remains in force for Candidate D and the current composite; Experiment 21 separately tested a bounded three-ticket alternative without changing that candidate;
- no candidate is promoted from search score alone;
- execution-cost stress is required before any V4.5 lock.

## Lock direction

A strong V4.5 lock candidate should move toward:

- roughly 20% or better 500 ms capture if achieved without fragility;
- materially reduced 500 ms -> 1 s degradation, with ~10% 1 s capture treated as an aspirational robustness level rather than a forced threshold;
- higher profit velocity than provisional Candidate D;
- no material PF or drawdown deterioration;
- improvement or containment of the weak 50-60% regime;
- positive cost-stress behavior.
